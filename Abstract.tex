\begin{abstract}
\justifying{
Cross-layer attacks against cyber-physical systems (CPS) can simultaneously corrupt observations, communication, control commands, and the federated knowledge used for post-attack recovery. This creates a recovery-integrity problem that is not addressed by conventional federated reinforcement learning (FRL): a globally shared recovery policy may be trained from heterogeneous clients whose updates are stale, incompatible, or Byzantine, and a poisoned policy may subsequently drive the physical plant outside its safe operating region. We therefore formulate \emph{trustworthy heterogeneous federated recovery}, in which both local observations and federated recovery knowledge are treated as potentially untrusted. We redesign AEFC as a certificate-gated federated recovery mechanism with three layers. First, heterogeneous clients retain subsystem-specific observation/action adapters and share only an aligned recovery backbone. Second, recovery knowledge is admitted to federation only when it is supported by local recovery value and safety certificates, while server aggregation uses Byzantine-resilient trimming rather than client-reported credibility as a trust root. Third, each learned action is passed through a robust safety-recovery shield before execution, separating learning utility from physical safety. Under bounded state/attack uncertainty and shield feasibility, we prove forward invariance of the safe set for arbitrary learned policy proposals, including proposals induced by model poisoning. For Top-$k$ communication, we derive an explicit deterministic contraction factor $\delta_k=k/d$ and combine it with a Byzantine aggregation deviation bound instead of assuming an unverifiable credibility bias. Existing IEEE 39-bus and IEEE 118-bus experiments are retained only for the performance and communication claims they directly support; the revised evaluation protocol further specifies certificate, adaptive-Byzantine, and privacy tests required for high-assurance deployment.
}
\end{abstract}
