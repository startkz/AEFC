\begin{abstract}
\justifying{
Federated recovery introduces an integrity dependency that is largely absent from conventional cyber--physical system (CPS) recovery formulations: a controller may need to restore a plant while its observations and control channel are corrupted, yet the distributed recovery knowledge used to adapt the controller may itself be stale, inconsistent, or adversarially poisoned. We formulate this problem as \emph{bounded-authority federated recovery}. Instead of treating federated knowledge as trusted state, AEFC treats it as untrusted evidence whose influence is explicitly bounded along three security-critical transitions: evidence-to-belief, belief-to-action authority, and accepted evidence-to-deployed-policy adaptation. AEFC combines a provenance-aware temporal belief update, a risk-aware recovery gate, trust-gated online policy adaptation, Byzantine-resilient federated coordination, and a robust physical execution shield. Under bounded physical corruption, bounded robust-aggregation error, contractive belief dynamics, bounded adaptation steps, uncertainty-set coverage, and shield feasibility, we establish bounded belief distortion, bounded false authorization, bounded deployed-policy deviation, finite $\varepsilon$-correction after poisoning under an explicit recovery condition, and policy-independent forward invariance of the physical safe set. Heterogeneous local adapters and deterministic Top-$k$ communication are retained as implementation mechanisms rather than security roots. In an orientation-safe native MathWorks R2024b IEEE 39-bus evaluation with seven methods, four corruption cells, and 20 matched seeds per cell, all methods remain free of episode-level safety violations under joint corruption, while AEFC reduces maximum belief distortion from 0.4978 to 0.0873 and maximum deployed-policy drift from 0.1407 to 0.0230 relative to FedRL; both paired comparisons remain significant after Holm correction ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}=1.14\times10^{-5}$). All numerical claims are generated from committed configurations and immutable per-seed traces.
}
\end{abstract}
