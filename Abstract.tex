\begin{abstract}
\justifying{
Cross-layer attacks against cyber-physical systems (CPS) can corrupt sensing, communication, and control channels at the same time, forcing recovery controllers to restore safe operation with partial observations and untrusted participants. Existing reinforcement learning (RL) recovery methods are still largely centralized, which exposes operational trajectories and scales poorly when subsystems are geographically distributed. This paper proposes a security-aware heterogeneous federated reinforcement learning (FRL) framework for collaborative resilient recovery under cross-layer attacks. The framework decomposes a CPS into subsystem-level agents, trains local recovery policies with prioritized multi-agent RL, and exchanges model updates without sharing raw trajectories. Its core mechanism, Advantage Execution--Federated Critic (AEFC), combines local safety adapters, recovery-state prototype alignment, high-advantage sparse update transmission, and shift-aware credibility aggregation. Local adapters keep subsystem-specific sensing and actuation private, prototype alignment stabilizes the shared recovery representation, sparse transmission limits communication to recovery-informative updates, and credibility aggregation reduces the impact of stale, noisy, or adversarial contributions. AEFC is positioned as a communication-efficient and empirically privacy-enhancing recovery mechanism, not as a formal differential-privacy or secure-aggregation protocol. The framework is evaluated on IEEE 39-bus and IEEE 118-bus power-grid CPS benchmarks implemented through Simulink--Python co-simulation. In these benchmarks, AEFC-FRL improves recovery accuracy by up to 15.3 percentage points over centralized MADDPG and reduces communication overhead by 45.8\% compared with standard FedRL, while maintaining stronger resilience under malicious updates.
}
\end{abstract}
