\section{CONCLUSION}
This paper reframes federated CPS recovery as a bounded-authority security problem. The central challenge is not merely that physical observations and control channels may be attacked or that federated learning may be poisoned; it is that corrupted recovery knowledge can propagate through belief formation, action authorization, online adaptation, and physical execution. AEFC therefore treats federated recovery knowledge as untrusted evidence rather than trusted controller state.

The revised design separates five guarantees. Provenance-aware temporal processing bounds belief distortion; the risk-aware gate bounds false authorization; trust-gated deployment bounds policy movement and enables explicit post-poisoning correction conditions; Byzantine-resilient coordination bounds the influence of malicious updates; and the robust physical execution shield preserves the safe set for arbitrary learned proposals when state/attack coverage and shield feasibility hold. This composition explains the security role of the federation instead of relying solely on a final safety filter.

Heterogeneous adapters, recovery-state alignment, and deterministic Top-$k$ remain useful for learning under subsystem diversity and communication constraints, but they are not treated as trust or privacy mechanisms. Likewise, empirical membership-inference behavior is not presented as a formal privacy guarantee.

The experimental contribution follows the same security discipline as the theory. In addition to the released mechanism-level evaluation, the final artifact contains an orientation-safe native MathWorks R2024b IEEE 39-bus experiment with seven methods, four corruption cells, and 20 matched seeds per cell, yielding 560 immutable native episodes. Under joint corruption, all compared methods remain free of episode-level safety violations and all recover in the observed horizon, so we do not attribute a binary safety advantage to AEFC in this operating envelope. The native evidence instead shows that AEFC-Full substantially reduces belief distortion and deployed-policy drift relative to FedRL while improving the minimum native safety margin; the corresponding paired comparisons remain significant after Holm correction. These results are generated from validated generator-local rotor-speed/\texttt{Pref} interfaces and raw per-seed traces rather than target curves.

Under this evidence model, the paper makes a strong but appropriately scoped TDSC claim: federated recovery knowledge may be adversarial, yet its authority over a safety-critical CPS can be bounded before it reaches the plant, and the resulting integrity benefits can be measured on a native IEEE 39-bus plant without overstating binary safety outcomes that are identical across the compared methods.
