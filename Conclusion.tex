\section{CONCLUSION}
This paper reframes federated CPS recovery as a bounded-authority security problem. The central challenge is not merely that physical observations and control channels may be attacked or that federated learning may be poisoned; it is that corrupted recovery knowledge can propagate through belief formation, action authorization, online adaptation, and physical execution. AEFC therefore treats federated recovery knowledge as untrusted evidence rather than trusted controller state.

The design separates five guarantees. Provenance-aware temporal processing bounds belief distortion; the risk-aware gate bounds false authorization; trust-gated deployment bounds policy movement and supports explicit post-poisoning correction conditions; Byzantine-resilient coordination limits the influence of malicious updates; and the robust physical execution layer preserves the safe set under the stated coverage and feasibility assumptions. This composition gives the federation a bounded security role rather than relying solely on a final safety filter.

Heterogeneous adapters, recovery-state alignment, and deterministic Top-$k$ remain useful for learning under subsystem diversity and communication constraints, but they are not treated as trust or privacy mechanisms. Likewise, the paper does not infer a formal privacy guarantee from data locality or sparse communication.

The experimental evidence follows the same separation. The released mechanism-level backend validates composition behavior, while the orientation-safe native MathWorks R2024b IEEE 39-bus evaluation executes seven methods, four corruption cells, and 20 matched seeds per cell, yielding 560 immutable native episodes. Under joint corruption, all compared methods remain free of episode-level safety violations and recover within the observed horizon, so no binary safety advantage is attributed to AEFC in this operating regime. The native evidence instead shows that AEFC-Full substantially reduces belief distortion and deployed-policy movement relative to FedRL while increasing the minimum native safety margin; the corresponding paired comparisons remain significant after Holm correction. These results are generated directly from validated generator-local rotor-speed/\texttt{Pref} interfaces and immutable per-seed traces.

Taken together, the analysis and evidence support a scoped conclusion: federated recovery knowledge may be adversarial, yet its authority over a safety-critical CPS can be bounded before it reaches the plant. The formal safety guarantee remains conditional on uncertainty-set coverage, bounded downstream corruption, and shield feasibility, while the native IEEE 39-bus results demonstrate measurable integrity benefits without overstating binary outcomes that are identical across the compared methods.
