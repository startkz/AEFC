\section{CONCLUSION}
This paper reframes federated CPS recovery as a bounded-authority security problem. The central challenge is not merely that physical observations and control channels may be attacked or that federated learning may be poisoned; it is that corrupted recovery knowledge can propagate through belief formation, action authorization, online adaptation, and physical execution. AEFC therefore treats federated recovery knowledge as untrusted evidence rather than trusted controller state.

The design separates five guarantees. Provenance-aware temporal processing bounds belief distortion; the risk-aware gate bounds false authorization; trust-gated deployment bounds policy movement and supports explicit post-poisoning correction conditions; Byzantine-resilient coordination limits the influence of malicious updates; and the robust physical execution layer preserves the safe set under the stated coverage and feasibility assumptions. This composition gives the federation a bounded security role rather than relying solely on a final safety filter.

Heterogeneous adapters, recovery-state alignment, and deterministic Top-$k$ remain useful for learning under subsystem diversity and communication constraints, but they are not treated as trust or privacy mechanisms. Likewise, the paper does not infer a formal privacy guarantee from data locality or sparse communication.

The experimental evidence follows the same separation. The authoritative orientation-safe MathWorks R2024b IEEE 39-bus Main Experiment executes seven methods, four corruption cells, and 20 matched seeds per cell, yielding 560 native episodes. Under joint corruption, all methods remain free of episode-level safety violations and recover within the observed horizon, so no binary safety advantage is attributed to AEFC in this operating regime. AEFC-Full instead reduces belief distortion and deployed-policy movement relative to FedRL while increasing the continuous native margin.

Five supplemental experiments clarify the component contributions and robustness of this result. The native attack-intensity sweep remains safe through twice the nominal joint-corruption magnitude, while PATBU reduces mean maximum belief distortion from 0.9889 to 0.1735 at $\gamma=2$. A targeted pre-gate stress shows that Full rejects all 580 dangerous attack-window proposals at $\alpha=1$, whereas no-Gate authorizes all of them and relies on the downstream shield; this directly separates action authority from physical invariance. A method-independent common-random-number calibration further shows that the extreme unconstrained FedRL behavior is substantially reduced by clipping and robust aggregation, yet AEFC retains significantly lower belief distortion and policy movement than RobustFedRL-Clip. The calibrated robust baseline recovers faster in the reduced-order backend, so the evidence supports bounded integrity rather than Pareto dominance over every utility metric. Cross-backend rankings agree on integrity metrics but not on physical margins, and local parameter sweeps reveal a non-fragile plateau rather than a uniquely optimal parameter point.

A separate extended native experiment follows the actual four-dimensional deployed controller coefficients for 176 post-poisoning samples against an offline same-operator clean-report shadow. It reproduces the original Main observations in the shared first 73 samples and uses 20 seeds each for three methods in knowledge-only and joint conditions. AEFC-Full has smaller post-attack parameter-reference deviation than the two comparisons, but none of the 120 native episodes achieves the pre-registered post-attack finite-$\varepsilon$ correction criterion. Consequently, the data do not establish the additional contraction condition required by the G3 theorem. These four-dimensional parameters must not be equated with complete neural-network training weights, and the native provenance path and physical-execution assurance assumptions remain separate limitations.

A further native held-out experiment, using distinct seeds 20--39 and no online clean-shadow or oracle adversary-identity inputs in the controller decision, evaluates report-consistency quarantine and locally checkpointed rollback on the same IEEE 39-bus plant. Against the tested knowledge-only and joint attacks, 19/20 seeds per condition transition from outside to inside a fixed checkpoint tolerance; the other seed is already inside it, so all 20 eventually maintain the tolerance. The same rollback incurs a measurable limitation: the final distance from the dynamic clean shadow and the native state-response magnitude are larger than in the unprivileged no-rollback arm, while continued deployment adaptation is disabled. We therefore identify a feasible \emph{degraded survival mode} under an intact, healthy commissioning checkpoint, not validated recovery toward the current clean policy, a generally contractive learner, or an independently certified robust physical controller.

Taken together, the analysis and evidence support a scoped conclusion: federated recovery knowledge may be adversarial, yet its authority over a safety-critical CPS can be bounded before it reaches the plant. The formal safety guarantee remains conditional on uncertainty-set coverage, bounded downstream corruption, and shield feasibility, while the native and fairness-calibrated experiments demonstrate distinct belief, authorization, and policy-integrity benefits without overstating binary outcomes that remain identical across methods in the Main operating regime.
