\section{CONCLUSION}
This paper presented a security-aware FRL framework for collaborative resilient recovery in distributed CPS under cross-layer attacks. The framework combines subsystem-level multi-agent reinforcement learning with an Advantage Execution--Federated Critic mechanism, enabling local recovery policies to share useful knowledge without exchanging raw trajectories. The design targets three requirements that are central to attack-time CPS recovery: sharing only an aligned recovery backbone across heterogeneous subsystems, transmitting recovery-informative updates over constrained links, and reducing the influence of unreliable or malicious client updates.

The empirical evaluation on the IEEE 39-bus and IEEE 118-bus power-grid benchmarks showed that the proposed method can reduce recovery time and communication overhead while maintaining resilience under subsystem heterogeneity and adversarial participation. The heterogeneity stress test further showed that local adapters and recovery-state prototypes help preserve recovery accuracy when observations, actuation constraints, and attack contexts differ across agents. The privacy-risk evaluation based on membership inference attacks suggests reduced attack efficacy under the tested protocol, but it is not a formal privacy guarantee. Sparse updates and clipped prototypes may still leak sensitive information through gradient reconstruction, and deployment in privacy-critical CPS should incorporate secure aggregation, clipping, and differential privacy when required.

The main validation remains concentrated on power-grid CPS, with only a small traffic-signal proof of concept used to check interface portability. Extension to full traffic-control benchmarks, industrial PLC networks, and other CPS domains requires domain-specific state encoders, safety metrics, actuation-delay models, and fallback controllers. Practical deployment also requires careful treatment of federated-round synchronization, edge-device compute limits, intermittent communication, stale updates, robust aggregation under high malicious-client ratios, and certified safety logic. These issues define the next step from simulation-grade resilient recovery toward deployable secure learning in real CPS.
