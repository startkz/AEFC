\section{SECURITY VALIDATION AND LIMITATIONS}

\subsection{Claim-to-Evidence Correspondence}
The formal guarantees and the executable evidence serve different purposes and are therefore interpreted separately.

\textbf{G1: bounded belief distortion.}
The mechanism-level traces record the clean counterfactual belief and the attacked belief, while the native controller records the resulting distortion diagnostic. These measurements test whether the implemented belief mechanism behaves consistently with the intended contraction structure. They do not replace the analytical assumption that the belief operator is contractive or establish a worst-case value of the bound in \eqref{eq:belief-bound}. The supplemental native PATBU ablation provides component-local evidence: as the joint-corruption scale increases from 0.5 to 2, Full remains consistently below no-PATBU in maximum belief distortion; at $\gamma=2$ the corresponding means are 0.1735 and 0.9889.

\textbf{G2: bounded false authorization.}
The experiments report an operational false-authorization rate based on the logged gate decision and a fixed dangerous-proposal indicator. As defined in the experimental section, this indicator is backend-specific. The empirical FAR therefore measures authorization behavior under the released attack configurations; it is not a direct estimate of the theorem parameter $\delta_g$, whose interpretation still depends on calibration or deterministic outer coverage of the risk bound. In the Main native experiment no dangerous proposal crosses the configured authorization boundary, so Full and no-Gate are indistinguishable in FAR. The targeted supplemental Gate stress is designed specifically to activate this boundary: at $\alpha=1$, Full rejects all 580 dangerous attack-window proposals across 20 seeds, while no-Gate authorizes all 580.

\textbf{G3--G4: bounded adaptation and post-poisoning correction.}
The mechanism-level backend records candidate/deployed-policy hashes and the deployed-policy movement, while the native path records adaptation decisions and the same movement metric. The released implementation does not provide a cryptographically certified rollback service. Consequently, the manuscript claims finite $\varepsilon$-correction only under the stated post-attack contraction condition and does not claim finite exact return of the policy parameters after poisoning. A new independent, extended native IEEE 39-bus diagnostic records the actual four-dimensional deployed coefficients and an offline same-operator clean-report shadow at every sample. Over 20 matched seeds for each of three methods in the knowledge-only and joint scenarios, every run initially exceeds the post-attack tolerance $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, but no run enters and maintains that tolerance for 20 consecutive samples during the subsequent 176 observations. For AEFC-Full, the mean parameter-reference error remains approximately 0.01005 (knowledge-only) and 0.01015 (joint), compared with approximately 0.0508/0.0505 for no-TGOPA and 0.1309/0.1304 for FedRL. In every AEFC-Full run there is at least one post-attack error increase. All runs report 176 accepted updates, so accepting updates does not itself imply contractive correction. The evaluation reference is a dynamic offline shadow on the same attacked plant trajectory, not the theorem's fixed certified recovery target. Thus this native evidence supports smaller deployment deviation, \emph{not} empirical satisfaction of the G3 finite-$\varepsilon$ correction premise. The fairness-calibrated E3 comparison strengthens the empirical interpretation: after common random numbers, clipping, and robust aggregation are controlled, AEFC-Full still reduces mean maximum policy movement from 0.0846 for RobustFedRL-Clip to 0.0339, although the calibrated robust baseline recovers faster in the reduced-order backend.

\textbf{G5: physical forward invariance.}
G5 is an analytical result for a feasible robust barrier-constrained execution layer under uncertainty-set and attack-set coverage. The native R2024b controller used in the 560-episode experiment implements a bounded sensitivity-based safety projection constructed from the validated $4\times4$ finite-difference mapping; it is not a full online CBF-QP solver. Accordingly, the native experiment reports observed safety-envelope violations and continuous native margins, but it is not presented as an empirical proof of the CBF forward-invariance theorem. This separation prevents an implementation-level safety observation from being conflated with the stronger analytical guarantee.

\subsection{Byzantine Aggregation and Sparse-Support Scope}
Byzantine clients may fabricate update values, timestamps, sparse indices, advantages, prototypes, confidence values, and other self-reported metadata. Such quantities are therefore never treated as authentication signals. The mechanism-level path uses a common-support robust aggregation interface and records the aggregate deviation from the honest center. The four-dimensional native controller receives complete local update vectors and applies robust shared-support selection before trimmed aggregation; its native results are not used to make a communication-compression claim.

For deterministic Top-$k$ compression, the implementation-level audit is based on
\begin{equation}
\frac{\|z_i^t-C_k(z_i^t)\|_2^2}{\|z_i^t\|_2^2}
\le1-\frac{k}{d}.
\end{equation}
This result characterizes compression error only. It does not authenticate a client, provide Byzantine provenance, or establish privacy.

\subsection{Native IEEE 39-Bus Evidence Boundary}
The authoritative native evidence is tied to the successful MathWorks R2024b workflow at commit \texttt{cc315ff522c90ce6adc09df3020472dd9ad820ea}. The workflow validates four generator-local rotor-speed/\texttt{Pref} pairs with a full-rank sensitivity matrix, executes 560 native episodes at $1/60$~s, uses orientation-safe \texttt{SimulationOutput} extraction with per-episode semantic guards, and releases immutable per-seed traces and summaries. The reduced-order 20-run-per-method experiment remains a mechanism-integration test and is not used as an IEEE 39-bus substitute.

The native evidence is deliberately interpreted within the configured attack envelope. Under joint corruption, all seven methods exhibit zero episode-level safety violations and 100\% recovery success across 20 matched seeds. The Main Experiment therefore does not support a claim that AEFC uniquely improves these binary outcomes in this operating regime. Its measured advantage is concentrated in belief integrity, deployed-policy movement, and the continuous native safety margin.

The supplemental experiments refine, rather than replace, this conclusion. The attack-intensity sweep remains free of observed native violations through $\gamma=2$, while AEFC-Full keeps the mean minimum native margin near 0.986 and limits the growth of belief distortion relative to FedRL and no-PATBU. The targeted Gate experiment provides a sharper causal decomposition: at $\alpha=1$, no-Gate authorizes all dangerous proposals but the downstream shield intervenes on the 29 attack-window samples per seed, so the plant still remains inside the observed envelope; Full rejects those proposals before they reach the shield. Thus zero plant violations in both cases do not imply that the Gate is redundant. They show that authorization control and downstream physical protection are distinct layers.

Cross-backend comparisons are also scoped to what the data support. Method rankings are aligned for belief distortion (Spearman $\rho=0.778$, $p=0.039$) and policy movement ($\rho=0.785$, $p=0.036$), but not for continuous physical margin ($\rho=0.038$, $p=0.935$). We therefore use the reduced-order backend to study mechanism composition and integrity failure modes, not to predict absolute native safety margins. The local parameter sweep is similarly interpreted as a plateau around the nominal $q_{\min}$, $\rho_{\max}$, and $\Delta_\theta$ values under the Main joint condition, not as evidence that those values are globally optimal.

The physical theorem remains conditional on uncertainty-set coverage and robust-shield feasibility. An adversary capable of imposing arbitrary unbounded downstream actuation lies outside the guarantee. IEEE 33-bus, IEEE 118-bus, and 8500-node quantitative results are not part of the released evidence used for the claims in this manuscript.

\subsection{Provenance and Privacy Scope}
Provenance authenticity requires a cryptographic authentication, trusted-time, or attestation mechanism appropriate to the deployment; a client-provided source identifier alone is not authenticated provenance. The current artifact models provenance validity but does not claim that this systems problem is solved by metadata fields themselves.

The AEFC security claim concerns integrity and physical safety rather than formal confidentiality. Data locality and sparse updates do not constitute differential privacy. No $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ privacy guarantee is claimed, and membership-inference behavior is not used as evidence for the bounded-authority security results.

These boundaries are part of the security model rather than post hoc qualifications. They identify which claims follow from analysis, which are supported by the released traces, and which remain deployment assumptions.
