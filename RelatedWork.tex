\section{RELATED WORK}
\subsection{Resilient Recovery and Secure Control of CPS}
CPS recovery has been studied using resilient estimation, robust control, optimization, game-theoretic methods, and learning-based decision making \cite{segovia2024survey,o2024towards,lu2024recovery,yuan2024incomplete,kim2022survey,niu2022verifying,yu2024intelligent,xin2025learning,zhong2024deep}. These approaches establish important recovery principles, but the information consumed by the recovery controller is frequently assumed to be sufficiently trustworthy. Our setting is different because sensing, control delivery, and the distributed learning process that adapts the controller may be attacked concurrently.

A key distinction is between learning robustness and physical safety. Even a statistically robust global model can produce an unsafe action in a safety-critical plant, while a model-independent safety constraint can preserve a physical invariant even when the performance controller is poor. We therefore use the learned policy only as a recovery proposal and place a robust execution contract at the physical authority boundary.

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning for CPS Recovery}
RL and multi-agent RL have been used for sequential recovery and distributed CPS control \cite{olowononi2020resilient,nguyen2021deep,zhang2021physical,jin2025comprehensive,10105656,10393887,yang2024adt,liu2024deadline,wang2023multi}. These methods motivate the actor--critic learner used by AEFC. However, a critic-estimated advantage is only a utility signal. Under corrupted observations or a poisoned critic, a large estimated advantage does not certify state correctness or physical safety. AEFC therefore separates recovery utility from authorization and execution safety.

\subsection{Federated Learning under Heterogeneity and Communication Constraints}
Federated learning reduces raw-data exchange but is affected by non-IID data, client drift, system heterogeneity, communication cost, and malicious updates \cite{kairouz2021advances,li2020federated}. Personalized and representation-aligned methods support decompositions in which a shared model is combined with client-specific components \cite{zhao2026fedara,park2026fedade,le2026feddap,wang2026f2dc}. AEFC follows this pattern by federating a recovery backbone while keeping incompatible observation and action adapters local.

Communication-efficient FL often applies sparsification or compressed updates \cite{jia2025comprehensive,11090013}. Compression does not authenticate a client and does not provide formal privacy. We therefore analyze deterministic Top-$k$ only as a communication mechanism and keep its residual explicit.

\subsection{Poisoning and Byzantine-Resilient Federated Learning}
Secure federated learning studies poisoning, backdoors, malicious clients, client screening, robust aggregation, secure computation, trusted execution, and differential privacy \cite{miao2024esfl,miao2024asyfl,mu2024feddmc,zhao2026sxgb,kato2023olive,liu2026dpfedadamw,zhao2026fedmop,patel2026pfdm}. Federated RL has also been investigated for distributed control and cyber defense \cite{qi2021federated,10908884,10483278,10536634,qi2025federated}. These studies demonstrate that federated coordination can remain useful under adversarial clients, but aggregation integrity alone does not specify how much physical authority a poisoned recovery model should receive.

\subsection{Gap: Recovery-Knowledge Authority}
The security gap addressed here lies in the propagation path from distributed evidence to the plant. Robust aggregation can bound a model update, and a robust safety filter can bound the final action, but the intermediate transitions---belief formation, action authorization, and online deployment---remain security-relevant. AEFC therefore treats federated recovery knowledge as untrusted evidence with \emph{bounded authority}. This composition, rather than Top-$k$, advantage estimation, prototype alignment, or a federated critic individually, is the security focus of the paper.
