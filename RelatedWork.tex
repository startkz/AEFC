\section{RELATED WORK}
Research on CPS recovery has developed along three closely related lines: resilient control design, reinforcement learning for sequential CPS decisions, and federated learning for distributed CPS data and models.

\subsection{Resilient Design for CPS}
Resilient design in CPS is commonly discussed from two perspectives: recovery strategies and recovery mechanisms \cite{segovia2024survey,o2024towards}. Recovery strategies define the target operating state and the restoration path after a disruption, whereas recovery mechanisms provide the control, optimization, or learning techniques used to realize that path.

Prior work distinguishes shallow recovery from deep recovery \cite{lu2024recovery}. Shallow recovery usually addresses localized disturbances, such as abnormal sensor readings, transient communication delays, or single-component failures, and is often implemented through redundancy, component substitution, or local repair. Deep recovery targets wider disruptions, including controller compromise, large network outages, or severe data loss, and typically requires longer-horizon optimization. Duan et al. \cite{11078001}, for example, proposed a two-layer network recovery strategy that decomposes a large network into core and peripheral subnetworks and restores them through a multi-level mechanism.

Many early recovery mechanisms rely on control theory or game theory. Robust control, observer reconstruction, and verification-based designs have improved resilience under structured uncertainty and attacks \cite{yuan2024incomplete,kim2022survey,niu2022verifying,yu2024intelligent}. Liang et al. \cite{xin2025learning} developed a learning-based passive resilient controller that confines the system state within a secure ellipsoidal region under covert deception attacks. Zhong et al. \cite{zhong2024deep} proposed a cascaded recovery model that coordinates absorption, survival, and recovery. Cheng et al. \cite{chen2019dynamic} modeled infrastructure defense as a two-person, three-stage game, and Yu et al. \cite{10552409} studied a modeling-control-design framework based on multi-agent game theory.

These methods provide useful recovery principles, but they are often tailored to specific models, single-scenario assumptions, or small-scale settings. Large CPS introduce data silos, heterogeneous local dynamics, and cross-layer coupling, which make purely centralized or model-specific designs harder to deploy. This motivates learning-based recovery methods that can adapt from interaction data while respecting distributed operation constraints.

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning for CPS}
Reinforcement learning has been used in CPS because it can optimize sequential decisions without requiring a complete analytical model of the environment \cite{olowononi2020resilient,nguyen2021deep}. Yan et al. \cite{10105656} studied replay attack detection with model-free reinforcement learning and treated attackers as proactive agents. Su et al. \cite{10393887} combined self-organizing maps with deep reinforcement learning for multivariate anomaly detection. Xue et al. \cite{yang2024adt} formulated anomaly-threshold selection as a Markov decision process and solved it with deep reinforcement learning. Liu et al. \cite{liu2024deadline} introduced an R-MDP formulation for deadline-aware CPS tasks and designed rewards that encourage timely behavior.

Because CPS are distributed and tightly coupled, a single learning agent may be unable to capture subsystem interactions. Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is therefore a natural fit for coordinated CPS control \cite{zhang2021physical,jin2025comprehensive}. Abiad et al. \cite{al2022joint} developed a MARL-based caching strategy for collaborative throughput optimization. Digital-twin-based multi-agent architectures have also been used for monitoring and anomaly detection \cite{lagache2024toward}. Wang et al. \cite{wang2023multi} modeled cross-layer routing in dynamic mobile networks as a decentralized partially observable Markov decision process and used graph neural network-based reinforcement learning. In transportation CPS, Xi et al. \cite{xi2025expert} proposed a MARL cluster-control algorithm for collaborative underwater control with centralized training and distributed execution.

Despite these advances, RL and MARL still face practical barriers in CPS recovery. Centralized training can require large volumes of operational trajectories, which is undesirable when subsystems are owned by different entities or connected through bandwidth-limited links. Frequent dense communication among agents can also strain the control network. These issues motivate a federated formulation in which learning signals are shared without exposing raw trajectories.

\subsection{Federated Learning for CPS}
Federated learning allows clients to train a shared model while keeping raw data local. Its known challenges include statistical heterogeneity, system heterogeneity, client drift, communication cost, and privacy leakage from shared updates \cite{kairouz2021advances,li2020federated}. These issues are amplified in CPS because measurements are operationally sensitive, distributed, and generated by safety-critical physical processes. Recent CPS-oriented studies have explored federated sensing, transportation, energy scheduling, maritime systems, and industrial edge intelligence \cite{quan2025federated,singh2025federated,qi2025federated,10707001}.

Two bodies of FL work are most relevant to this paper. The first concerns secure and communication-efficient aggregation. ESFL improves backdoor resistance through efficient secure filtering \cite{miao2024esfl}, while Byzantine-robust and privacy-preserving schemes use blockchain-assisted coordination, malicious-client detection, TEEs, oblivious learning, or differential privacy \cite{miao2024asyfl,mu2024feddmc,zhao2026sxgb,kato2023olive}. Other CPS and edge-FL studies reduce communication through adaptive aggregation, edge-cloud resource allocation, incentive mechanisms, over-the-air aggregation, or compression \cite{su2025joint,fu2024distributed,nariman2025communication,jia2025comprehensive,11090013}. These methods provide important infrastructure, but they usually optimize supervised or generic training objectives. In attack-time CPS recovery, the value of an update also depends on whether it encodes an action that restores safety.

The second body concerns heterogeneous and privacy-aware FL. Personalized and representation-aligned methods, including FedARA, Fed-ADE, FedDAP, and F$^2$DC, separate shared and local components or use prototypes to handle client-specific domains \cite{zhao2026fedara,park2026fedade,le2026feddap,wang2026f2dc}. Privacy-focused methods such as DP-FedAdamW, FedMOP, and personalized federated diffusion training further show that data locality and sparsification are not substitutes for differential privacy, secure aggregation, or comparable mechanisms \cite{liu2026dpfedadamw,zhao2026fedmop,patel2026pfdm}. These studies inform the design and the privacy boundary of AEFC-FRL, but they do not solve sequential resilient recovery under coupled physical, sensing, communication, and control-layer attacks.

Federated reinforcement learning (FRL) extends FL to sequential decision-making and has been studied for distributed control, intelligent networking, malware mitigation, machine unlearning, predictive maintenance, and energy-system scheduling \cite{qi2021federated,10908884,10483278,10536634,qi2025federated}. CyberForce is particularly relevant because it evaluates poisoning resilience in an IoT security setting \cite{10908884}. Runtime detection for backdoored DRL agents also shows that learned policies can become attack surfaces \cite{zhang2025runtime}. These works motivate secure FRL, but most do not address cross-layer CPS recovery with heterogeneous observation/action spaces and recovery-time communication limits.

We therefore position this work as heterogeneous FRL rather than vertical federated learning. Classical vertical FL assumes feature-partitioned data with aligned sample identities \cite{liu2024vertical}; here, agents interact with different local environments and generate different trajectories. AEFC-FRL shares only an aligned recovery backbone, while local adapters keep subsystem-specific observation and action mappings private. Compared with existing CPS-oriented FL and FRL methods, the key difference is not any single primitive. Top-$\kappa$ sparsification, representation alignment, and credibility weighting all have precedents. AEFC-FRL changes their optimization target: the sparse mask depends on recovery advantage, the shared representation is aligned through recovery-state prototypes, and the aggregation weight combines critic-informed utility with drift. This makes the method a security-aware recovery mechanism rather than a generic compression scheme applied unchanged to CPS.
