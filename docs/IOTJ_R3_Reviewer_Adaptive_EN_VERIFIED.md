# IoT-J R3 — Reviewer comment: adaptive credibility maximization

We agree that random, scaled, sign-flipped, or fixed joint forgeries do not establish resistance to rule-aware adaptive weight manipulation. We therefore implemented attacks that jointly optimize sparse upload values/support, forged recovery utility and prototype vectors under the unchanged server validation and clipping rule. The attacker uses its own current upload, previously broadcast global changes/prototypes and its own earlier public aggregation weight, but no current honest-client private uploads or held-out recovery labels.

The complete audited IEEE39 matrix uses N=10, M=2, five seeds (90–94), 1000 training episodes and ten held-out evaluations per seed, comparing clean, fixed joint spoofing, weight-only and damage-aware objectives across strengths 0.25/0.5/1/2. Utility-only, prototype-only and update-only interventions are separately tested at strength 1. We report paired held-out restored-load and safe-dwell degradation, accepted malicious-client fractions, aggregate malicious weight, roundwise distributions and per-seed bootstrap intervals.

The largest *observed mean* paired restored-load shortfall among adaptive conditions was 2.82 percentage points for adaptive_weight_050 (uncapped), with mean attack-phase malicious weight 0.998; under the separately labeled 0.20 per-client cap, it was 0.43 percentage points for adaptive_meta_100, with mean malicious weight 0.400. These are empirical outcomes on the specified quasi-static AC backend, not worst-case bounds, dynamic-grid safety certificates or Byzantine tolerance proofs. The intensity=2 condition can be constrained by clipping and utility caps, so more nominal intensity need not mean more effective submitted manipulation.

We also distinguish the cited BRPPFL Byzantine/cloud-edge setting from our recovery-control setting, VFL+ vertical learning from our heterogeneous shared-backbone setting, and the two NOMA physical-layer secrecy works from federated poisoning.
